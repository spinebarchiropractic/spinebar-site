Auto Accident Injuries

Symptoms after a collision may develop immediately or become more noticeable later. A documented evaluation can help clarify how the neck, back, joints, and soft tissues are functioning after the event.

Clinically reviewed by Dr. Arthur Chakrian, DC · Last reviewed September 22, 2026

Understanding the pattern

Symptoms can have more than one possible source. An in-person assessment helps connect what you feel with your history, movement, and relevant examination findings.

Common reasons people seek an evaluation

  • Neck stiffness, headaches, or upper-back discomfort after a collision
  • Lower-back pain or reduced movement following impact
  • Symptoms that affect sleep, work, driving, or daily activity

What your first visit may include

  • Detailed accident and symptom history with appropriate documentation
  • Orthopedic, neurologic, and movement screening based on presentation
  • Referral for imaging or medical evaluation when clinically indicated
Medical note: This page is educational and is not a diagnosis. Seek urgent medical care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, new weakness, loss of bowel or bladder control, saddle numbness, major trauma, chest pain, or other emergency concerns.
